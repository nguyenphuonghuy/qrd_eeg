\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Knowledge distillation based on relations among samples is an effective
approach for motor imagery decoding. However, prior work commonly fixes
the relation-formation strategy empirically. This study proposes a
structured relational distillation framework, evaluated under
closed-loop control constraints. This framework treats the
relation-formation strategy and the similarity kernel function,
classical or quantum, as two independent design parameters. Twelve
configurations were compared on PhysionetMI (103 subjects, five
independent seeds, $n=5$). Distillation provides a large and stable
benefit. Accuracy increased from 74.93 to 81.49 percent on PhysionetMI,
and this result was replicated on a second independent dataset
(Cho2017, 49 subjects). However, the difference among specific
mechanisms is smaller than the noise from random seeds (standard
deviation 0.459 versus 0.750 percentage points), and the ranking from
a single run is nearly uncorrelated with the ranking over five seeds
($\rho = 0.14$). The study additionally proposes a control-oriented
evaluation procedure. This procedure uses information transfer rate
and the inference-time budget instead of accuracy alone, together
with a real inference-time measurement on embedded hardware. Under
this approach, the quantum kernel function yields a difference of
0.51 percentage points from the two classical kernel functions, close
to the pre-specified equivalence margin, so there is not yet enough
data to conclude in either direction. The training cost of the
quantum branch is about 13 times higher and increases nonlinearly
with the number of qubits. This makes large-scale training infeasible
on a shared single-GPU infrastructure. The evaluation framework and
the resulting quantitative benchmarks provide a reference basis for
more complex quantum designs tested later.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Brain--computer interface; knowledge distillation; motor imagery decoding; quantum machine learning; controlled evaluation.
